Calculators

RICE prioritization

Score each idea by Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort. Use it to make your assumptions comparable and debatable, not as an automatic decision. (See Day 11.)

IdeaReach (users / quarter)ImpactConfidenceEffort (person-months)Row

Ranking (highest score first)

Impact uses Intercom's scale (3 massive → 0.25 minimal). Confidence below 50% usually means "do discovery first". Effort includes design, engineering and QA time.

ICE scoring

A faster, rougher alternative to RICE for growth experiments: rate Impact, Confidence and Ease from 1 to 10. This calculator averages the three; some teams multiply instead, which usually produces a similar ranking.

WSJF (Weighted Shortest Job First)

WSJF, as used in SAFe, divides the Cost of Delay by job size. Cost of Delay = business value + time criticality + risk reduction / opportunity enablement. Score each column relative to the other items using the 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 20 scale.

JobBusiness valueTime criticalityRisk reduction / opportunityJob sizeRow

Ranking

WSJF favours small, urgent jobs. That is its purpose, but sanity-check the result: a large strategic bet can rank low simply because it is large.

A/B test sample size

How many users each variant needs before you can reliably detect the effect you care about, for a conversion-rate metric.

Formula: n per variant = (zα/2 + zβ)² × [p₁(1−p₁) + p₂(1−p₂)] ÷ (p₂ − p₁)². This is a standard normal approximation; dedicated calculators that use pooled variance can differ by a few percent. Decide the sample size before starting, don't stop early because results look significant (peeking inflates false positives), and run whole weeks. (See Day 20.)

Unit economics (subscription)

Lifetime value, LTV : CAC and payback for a simple monthly subscription. Use the same currency for every input.

LTV here = ARPU × gross margin ÷ monthly churn, which assumes constant churn and no expansion revenue. Payback = CAC ÷ monthly gross profit per customer. Ratios like "LTV : CAC of 3 : 1" are rules of thumb, not laws; early cohorts and discounts can make both numbers misleading.

Customer interview script — learn about past behaviour, not opinions 
CUSTOMER INTERVIEW SCRIPT (30 min)

Goal of this interview: [the one thing you want to learn]

INTRO (2 min)
- Thanks. We're learning how people [do the job]. No right answers;
  we're not selling anything. OK to take notes?

CONTEXT (5 min)
- Tell me about your role / your week when it comes to [topic].

THE LAST TIME (15 min)  -- dig into one real, recent example
- Tell me about the last time you [did the job]. What happened?
- What triggered it? What did you try first?
- What was the hardest part? Why?
- What did that cost you (time, money, stress)?
- What tools or workarounds do you use today? What do you dislike?
- Have you looked for a better way? What happened?

WRAP-UP (5 min)
- Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?
- Who else should I talk to?

AVOID: "Would you use...?", "Do you like this idea?", leading questions,
pitching your solution, and future hypotheticals.
After: write the 3 most important things you learned within 1 hour.

OUTCOME: [one measurable product outcome, e.g. +10% week-4 retention]
|
+-- Opportunity 1: [a customer need or pain, in their words]
|   +-- Solution 1a: ...
|   |   +-- Assumption test: [riskiest assumption] -> [cheap test]
|   +-- Solution 1b: ...
|
+-- Opportunity 2: ...
|   +-- Solution 2a: ...
|

Rules of thumb: opportunities come from interviews, not brainstorming;
compare at least 3 solutions for the opportunity you pick; test
assumptions before building.
